Learning SciVal: Faculty Development in Canvas

Learning SciVal is a self-paced Canvas course designed to help Nova Southeastern University faculty explore research activity, interpret scholarly metrics, and communicate research impact. Its six-module framework covers platform navigation, research metrics, institutional analysis, researcher profiles and groups, reporting, and responsible use of SciVal.

My work focuses on adapting Elsevier’s SciVal training resources for NSU faculty through a revised course outline, customized homepage, university branding, and practical learning goals. The course pairs instructional content with practice activities, discussions, knowledge checks, and quizzes to support application.

The design emphasizes clear navigation, flexible participation, and connections to faculty research needs, including examining collaboration patterns and preparing research summaries. It also encourages learners to interpret metrics in context and alongside professional judgment.

Evaluate Sources with CARDIO

A self-paced tutorial that teaches computing students to judge whether a source is credible, using the six CARDIO criteria: Currency, Authority, Relevance, Documentation, Information Type, and Objectivity. Learners work through one criterion at a time using a real practice article, answer guided reflection prompts, and complete a knowledge check before they can move on.

The module opens with visual primers on the information cycle and peer review, embeds a video on lateral reading, and ends by sending learners to the required course quiz, so the practice and the graded assessment stay aligned.

Database Keyword Search Builder

A six-step guided tool that turns a vague research idea into a working database search. Learners define their topic (What), angle (Why), and population (Who), see those pieces combine into a research question, add synonyms, and receive ready-to-paste search strings formatted for both multi-box databases and single-box search engines.

It breaks a technical skill that often overwhelms beginners into small, confident steps and gives learners something they can use immediately.

Library of Things: Collectible Bookmark Campaign

Most people think of an academic library as books, databases, and study space. To show students and visitors everything else they can borrow, I created a series of six collectible bookmarks for the Alvin Sherman Library’s Library of Things, each featuring a different item, from power banks and graphing calculators to science models and an office supply kit.

The concept

Instead of a standard informational handout, I borrowed ideas from trading cards and sticker collections. Each bookmark is numbered, No. 1 through 6, and gives its item playful “stats” and a “special ability,” so the set feels like something worth keeping and completing. The back of every bookmark carries the practical message: what the Library of Things offers and where to explore the collection.

Design process

I set the creative direction for a soft, storybook-style look: pastel colors, rounded characters, simple backgrounds, NSU branding, and a recurring shark plushie mascot. The original Power Bank artwork became the reference for the whole series. When later concepts drifted toward a more realistic or anime-like style, I brought them back to that original direction, choosing consistency over complexity and recognizability over realism.

The illustrations were created with generative AI image tools under my direction. I wrote and refined the prompts, compared versions, and rejected anything that broke the established style, then laid out each bookmark with its item stats, special ability, and call to action. Treating AI as a design tool, rather than letting it set the look, is what kept six separate images reading as one collection.

The full collection. Tap any bookmark to view it full size.

Getting students to explore

Rather than stacking all six designs at one desk, I split them across three service points, two per desk, so collecting the full set means visiting different parts of the library and meeting the staff there.

CirculationNo. 2 Graphing Calculator, No. 4 Whiteboards
Academic Reference ServicesNo. 3 Wired Earbuds, No. 5 Scientific Models & Kits

I also planned the physical display at the Academic Reference Services suite entrance and coordinated with staff to keep the bookmarks organized and available.
